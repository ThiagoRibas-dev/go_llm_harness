## 📚 1. Temporal Continuity: Baddeley's Model of Working Memory (1974)
* **The Cognitive Science Theory:** Alan Baddeley and Graham Hitch revolutionized cognitive psychology by demonstrating that "short-term memory" is not a flat storage bin, but a highly active, multi-component **Working Memory System**.
* **Episodic Buffer (The Chronological Axis):** Baddeley introduced the **Episodic Buffer** as the specific cognitive "slice" responsible for binding information from the phonological loop and visuospatial sketchpad into coherent, chronological, and linear "episodes" across time.
* **Key References:**
  * Baddeley, A. D., & Hitch, G. (1974). *Working Memory*. In *Psychology of Learning and Motivation* (Vol. 8, pp. 47-89). Academic Press.
  * Baddeley, A. (2000). *The episodic buffer: a new component of working memory?* *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 4(11), 417-423.

## ⛓️ 2. Causality & Logic: Kahneman's Dual-Process Theory & The Wason Selection Task (1966)
* **The Cognitive Science Theory:** Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky formalized **Dual-Process Theory**, dividing human cognition into two distinct operating modes:
  * **System 1 (Fast, Associative):** Highly parallel, pattern-matching, and fluid. It handles our *Stylistic-Prose* and superficial associative tasks.
  * **System 2 (Slow, Sequential):** Step-by-step, highly logical, and causal. It requires significant computational energy and handles the *Causal-Logical* axis.
* **The Cognitive Limit (Wason Selection Task):** Peter Wason proved that humans are notoriously bad at abstract conditional logic (deducing *"If P, then Q"*). However, when the exact same logical task is framed in terms of social rules (causal world boundaries), our cognitive capacity spikes, proving that our *Causal-Logical* axis is deeply intertwined with our *Semantic-World* models.
* **Key References:**
  * Kahneman, D. (2011). *Thinking, Fast and Slow*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
  * Wason, P. C. (1966). *Reasoning*. In *New Horizons in Psychology* (pp. 135-151). Penguin.

## 🗺️ 3. Ontological & Spatial Mapping: Kant's Transcendental Idealism (1781) & Kosslyn's Image Scanning (1978)
* **The Philosophy of Mind (Kant):** Long before computers, Immanuel Kant argued in his *Critique of Pure Reason* that human minds do not passively record reality. Instead, our minds have built-in, *a priori* "forms of intuition"—specifically **Space** and **Time**—and the category of **Causality**, which act as the absolute, non-negotiable coordinates through which we project and construct all cognitive experiences.
* **The Cognitive Neuroscience (Kosslyn):** Stephen Kosslyn conducted landmark mental-image-scanning experiments. He proved that when humans mentally navigate a map, the time it takes to "scan" from Point A to Point B is linearly proportional to the physical distance on the map, proving that the mind maintains a high-fidelity **spatial-ontological workspace (The Semantic-World Axis)** in-memory.
* **Key References:**
  * Kant, I. (1781). *Critique of Pure Reason* (Kritik der reinen Vernunft).
  * Kosslyn, S. M., Ball, T. M., & Reiser, B. J. (1978). *Visual images preserve metric spatial information: Evidence from studies of image scanning.* *Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 4(1), 47.

## 🧠 4. Social Modeling & Theory of Mind: Dunbar's Orders of Intentionality (1998)
* **The Cognitive Science Theory:** David Premack and Guy Woodruff coined the term **"Theory of Mind"** (ToM) in 1978 during primate studies.
* **Orders of Intentionality (The Behavioral-Psychological Axis):** Robin Dunbar formalized the scaling limits of ToM. He showed that social cognition scales in "orders of intentionality":
  * 1st Order: *I believe X.*
  * 2nd Order: *I believe that you think Y.*
  * 3rd Order: *I believe that you think that she wants Z.*
* **The Brain Size Barrier:** Dunbar’s neocortex scaling studies showed that due to the physical parameter volume of the human prefrontal cortex, **healthy adult humans are computationally capped at 5 orders of intentionality**. Attempting to process 6th or 7th order intentionality causes immediate cognitive breakdown, directly mirroring how smaller parameter LLMs suffer "representational collapse" on complex character dynamics.
* **Key References:**
  * Premack, D., & Woodruff, G. (1978). *Does the chimpanzee have a theory of mind?* *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 1(4), 515-526.
  * Dunbar, R. I. M. (1998). *The Social Brain Hypothesis*. *Evolutionary Anthropology: Issues, News, and Reviews*, 6(5), 178-190.

## 🎭 5. Style vs. Meaning: Chomskyan Syntactic Modularity (1957) & Fodor's Modularity of Mind (1983)
* **The Linguistic Modular Theory (Chomsky):** Noam Chomsky famously proved that the human mind processes **Syntax (form/style)** completely independently from **Semantics (meaning)**. He proved this with his legendary, syntactically perfect but semantically empty sentence:
  > *"Colorless green ideas sleep furiously."*
  This absolute separation is the primary academic foundation of our division between the *Stylistic-Prose* and *Semantic-World* axes.
* **The Modularity of Mind (Fodor):** Jerry Fodor argued that the mind is comprised of highly specialized, isolated, and "encapsulated" cognitive modules (e.g., the language faculty, the visual processing unit) that execute in parallel, feeding their output into a central, non-modular global processor.
* **Key References:**
  * Chomsky, N. (1957). *Syntactic Structures*. Mouton de Gruyter.
  * Fodor, J. A. (1983). *The Modularity of Mind: An Essay on Faculty Psychology*. MIT Press.
